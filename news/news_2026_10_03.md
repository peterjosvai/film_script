# PDF export... and the ultimate problem: pagination

hi, 

I have come to a decision (solution):  
exporting to PDF should be done independently from film_script conversion...  
aka from **fsc_conv**...  

It should be done in another application,  
for it is another conversion... 

Making a combo-application is not wise...
especially because an export to PDF can really take long...
which involves "threading" (handling different processing threads),
so the application wouldn't be frozen for a time... 
This is a different approach...

## The ultimate problem: (continued) / (more) and CONT'D

This is a feature which every screenwriting application does seamlessly...  
People are more annoyed than grateful.  
However, it is like salt: if you don't have it, you might badly miss it.  

The problem:  
You don't know in advance where the page will break...  
when the conversion is done, it is too late to think about adding "(more)" or CONTINUED at the end and the beginning of one page and the next, respectively...

It has to be done beforehand...  
counting the lines, literally...  
and measuring space   

and "reflow" all the pages all the time... 

While...

... While it is actually indicated, already:  
by the page numbers that are at the top ... 

### Think about it:

you read something on page 38...  
then turn the page, now it's 39...

Isn't that clear that it is a continuation to the previous page?

To put a "CONTINUED" notation at the end of a page serves only the purpose of an insurance...  
making sure that the actors and the crew all know that the scene hasn't reached its end yet...
 that there's more, still... in that scene... 
 
### the original case

The "(more)" and "CONTINUED" notations are best when using a typewriter...  
that is, when they are added manually...  
that is, when the writer thought that it would be better to put that indication there...  

Let's also bear in mind that with typewriters, pages can be shuffled... or just fall out...  

Also they re photocopied manually... 

### the present of screenwriting

Well, the present already slopes toward paperless filmscript / screenplay management.
In an age, in which musicians in an orchestra, playing period-faithful, historically built baroque instruments use tablets to read the sheet music,  
it is not too much to ask from film people, shooting "on" "digital", to use "digital" filmscripts instead of paper...  
right?

Just think of the screenplay update process...  by production assistants...  early in the morning...  
And think of today's "cloud" collaboration models...  
which we all speak "natively" these days...  
even though we used to use paper back in the day...  

A screenplay should be like an ebook... in **epub** format,   
with each scene on a new "page"...  
unless it is a montage...  

The assumed cinematic time can be indicated in a more sophisticated way than using the page numbers...   

## So... 
So, although I too like challenges (by which I don't mean hard tasks but tasks which probe your creativity),   
I don't think it would be a clever idea to force our "system" to stamp "(more)" and "CONTINUED" signs on page ends and beginnings...  

I will just add a simple application: fsc_pdf (based on Weasyprint), and that would be it... 

**as to Weasyprint...**

It works beautifully, to the utmost...  
Translates everything according to the latest HTML5 standards... 
which means cutting edge...  

It takes but 1 line of Python code to produce a perfect PDF... 
(more could be added, also there's a lot to be explored,   
but one line is already suffice...)

So, let's take a moment to festively dump the idea of "more and continued" stamps :)
